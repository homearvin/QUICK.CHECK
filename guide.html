Step-by-step guide

How to use QuickCheck

A simple, click-by-click guide. Do the parts in order the first time — after that, checking a set of papers takes only a minute.

💡

You can't get lost. QuickCheck always shows you the next button to press, usually a dark or highlighted button. Whenever you're unsure what to do — look for that button and click it.

What's in this guide

  1. Open QuickCheck & put in your key
  2. Make your class list
  3. Make a test
  4. Type the answer key
  5. Print the answer sheets
  6. Check the papers (take photos)
  7. See the results
1

Open QuickCheck & put in your key

You only do this the very first time.

  1. Double-click QuickCheck. If a blue box says "Windows protected your PC," click More info then Run anyway — this is normal and safe.
  2. The QuickCheck window opens. Keep it open while you work; closing it stops the app.
  3. It asks for your license key. Choose your .qckey file and click Activate.
    (Tip: if the .qckey file is in the same folder as QuickCheck, a one-click Activate button appears.)
  4. Go to the Settings tab on the left and type your name and school, then click Save.

2

Make your class list

So QuickCheck knows the names of your students.

In the left menu, click Class Lists

  1. Click Add a class list (or Create).
  2. Choose the kind of list:
    • Simple scorebook — the easiest. Just a list of names. Pick this if you only want to record scores.
    • DepEd e-class record — records straight into the official DepEd Excel format.
  3. Type the Grade and Section, and a name for the list (example: "Grade 8 Mabini — Science").
  4. Click Next: add students.
  5. Type your students' names, one per line (for a Simple scorebook). Then click Next: review.
  6. Check the details, then click Create class record(s). Your class list is ready.
3

Make a test

One test = one quiz or exam.

In the left menu, click Check Papers

  1. Click Create test.
  2. Give the test a name (example: "Quiz 1 — Science").
  3. Choose the class you just made from the list.
  4. Type how many questions there are (example: 20), and the choices (example: A–D).
  5. Click Save.
4

Type the answer key

Tell QuickCheck the correct answers.

  1. On your test, click Set the answer key.
  2. For each question number, tap the correct letter (A, B, C, or D).
  3. Click Save.
Watch the button change. Once the key is saved, the test's button changes to Check papers — that's your next step. Just follow it.
5

Print the answer sheets

The special sheets students shade their answers on.

In the left menu, click Print Sheets

  1. Choose your test.
  2. Print the sheets — either one blank sheet to photocopy, or a named sheet for each student.
  3. Give them to your students. They shade the circle for each answer.
Tell students: shade the circles darkly and fully with pencil. Light or half-shaded marks are harder to read.
6

Check the papers

This is the part that saves you hours. There are two ways — pick one.

Way A — using your phone (best for taking photos)

On the computer, click Phone Access

  1. It shows an address and a PIN. Make sure your phone and computer are on the same Wi-Fi.
  2. On your phone's browser, type that address, then enter the PIN.
  3. On the phone, open Check Papers → your test → tap 📷 Take a photo and snap each sheet.
Way B — using the computer
  1. Click Check Papers → your test → Check papers.
  2. Click to upload the photos you already have on your computer.
Then — review each paper (same on phone or computer)
  1. You'll see the photo and the answers QuickCheck read. Fix any wrong ones by tapping the correct letter.
  2. Choose the student's name from the list.
  3. Click Confirm & next. Repeat for every paper.
  4. When all papers are done, click Save all.
Safety check: if a student already has a saved score, QuickCheck warns you before replacing it (it shows the old and new score). Read it, then confirm — so no score is ever changed by accident.
7

See the results

Scores, the class average, and which questions were hardest.

In the left menu, click Results

  1. Pick your test.
  2. See each student's score, the class average, and the hardest questions.
  3. Use the buttons to download the scores (Excel) or a report (PDF).
That's the whole cycle: class list → test → answer key → print → check → results. Next time, you skip straight to printing and checking.
